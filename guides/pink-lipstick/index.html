The pink lipstick guide

Pink lipstick, every kind of it

We measured 24,097 lipsticks. 5,235 of them are pink, about 1 in 5. It's the biggest, fuzziest family in the aisle. Here's how to tell the pinks apart.

A common myth

Pink is not light red

Mix red with white and you'd expect pink. You get coral. At every depth, shades named pink sit cooler than shades named red, closer to blue.

Pink is its own direction on the color wheel, not a paler red.

So what makes a pink pink?

Imagine every lipstick color mapped out with pure gray at the center. For a shade to truly read as pink, it has to pass three tests:

  1. 1The right direction. Pink sits in a wide slice on the map, spanning from just short of purple to just short of coral.
  2. 2Enough color. If a shade sits too close to gray, it drifts into mauve or greige. Pale ballet pinks are the exception: the lighter the shade, the less saturation it needs to still feel pink.
  3. 3Light enough for its warmth. Cool pinks can go deep and moody, stretching all the way to deep raspberry. But as pink warms up, it must stay lighter; otherwise, it turns into rose-brown, nude, or terracotta. Deep, vivid warmth belongs firmly to red's territory.

The pink-nude line

Is it pink… or is it nude?

Soft, warm pinks can fall into a blurry space between pink and nude. There’s no official rulebook for where one ends and the other begins, so we use the names brands give their own shades.

  1. 1We group lipsticks by color.
  2. 2We look at how brands name those shades. Are they usually called pink or nude?
  3. 3Whichever name is used most often is the label we use. If most brands call a color pink, we call it pink. If most call it nude, we call it nude.

Sheer pinks

The paler the pink, the sheerer it gets

More than two in three pale pinks are sheer: balms, glosses and lip oils. Together they outnumber pale bullet lipsticks four to one. Across all pinks, it's the other way round.

Worth knowing: a sheer product's photo shows it in the tube or as a light swatch, not as worn. Many pale pinks look pale partly because they're sheer, and they'll show more of your own lip color.

Metallic & frost pinks

The frosted pink is back

The shimmery pinks of the '90s and 2000s are making a comeback, now as foils and metallics. 230 pinks have a shimmer finish (4.4% of all pinks) and about a third of them say frost, metallic or pearl right in the name.

Splurge or save?

No kind of pink costs more. In 89% of product lines, every shade costs the same, and within a brand, every kind of pink costs about the same. What money changes is the range: luxury brands make warmer pinks.
